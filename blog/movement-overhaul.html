Complete Mechanical Movement Servicing

By Jean-Luc Dubois • 8 Min Read

A high horology mechanical movement contains over 250 micro-components working in continuous harmony: gears, pinions, escapement levers, balance springs, and jewel bearings. Over four to five years of continuous operation, synthetic micro-lubricants naturally dry out and oxidize. Operating a mechanical watch without fresh lubrication creates metal-on-jewel friction that causes permanent pivot wear. A complete **movement overhaul** involves complete disassembly, ultrasonic chemical cleaning, pivot inspection, precise multi-viscosity relubrication, and COSC chronometer calibration. This guide details master watchmaker servicing steps.

1. The Disassembly & Ultrasonic Cleaning Process

Master servicing stages:

  • 1. Deconstruction: The movement is carefully demounted component by component under high-power stereoscopic loupes.
  • 2. 4-Stage Ultrasonic Wash: Movement plates, bridges, and wheels are placed in specialized basket jars and submerged in ultrasonic cleaning solvent tanks to strip old oils and micro-debris.
  • 3. Synthetic Relubrication: Watchmakers apply 4 distinct synthetic Swiss Moebius oils—thin oil for high-speed escapement impulses, thick grease for keyless mainspring winding gears.

"Watch servicing is preventive medicine for horology. Fresh micro-lubricants ensure your mechanical movement runs smoothly for another five years."

2. COSC Timing Calibration

Once reassembled, the movement is mounted on a electronic Witschi timing machine to measure beat error, amplitude (280° - 310°), and rate in five vertical and horizontal positions before casing.
