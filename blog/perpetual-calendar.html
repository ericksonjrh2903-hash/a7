Perpetual Calendar Leap Year Tracking

By Jean-Luc Dubois • 8 Min Read

Standard date watches require manual calendar corrections five times a year (at the end of February, April, June, September, and November). An **Annual Calendar** automatically handles 30 and 31-day months but still requires manual adjustment on March 1st. The ultimate mechanical calendar complication is the **Perpetual Calendar** (Quantième Perpétuel)—a mechanical computer built with levers, cams, and a 48-month program wheel that automatically accounts for 28, 30, and 31-day months, as well as February 29th during leap years. This guide details perpetual calendar mechanics.

1. The 48-Month Mechanical Program Wheel

Understand how mechanical memory operates without microchips:

  • The 48-Month Cam: A notched wheel that rotates once every four years (48 months). Deep notches represent 28-day Februaries, a shallow notch represents 29-day leap year February, and high points represent 31-day months.
  • Grand Date Jumper Springs: Instantaneously advance date, day, month, and moonphase discs at midnight in a fraction of a second.

"A perpetual calendar is a mechanical program recorded in steel and gold. It remembers century leap cycles without a single microchip."

2. Photorealistic 122-Year Moonphase Accuracy

Standard moonphase watches use a 59-tooth gear that accumulates a 1-day error every 2.5 years. Our Perpetual Calendars utilize a 135-tooth precision gear train that operates accurately for 122 years before requiring a single day correction.
